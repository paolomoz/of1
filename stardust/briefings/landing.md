# Intent

Make the visitor feel something before they understand anything. This page creates desire — the emotional pull that makes someone request early access before they've read a single feature spec. It should feel like nothing else in martech: bold, editorial, manifesto energy.

# Audience

Forward-thinking marketing leaders and experience directors who are tired of segmentation theater. They arrive from word-of-mouth, social shares, or direct links. They've tried Dynamic Yield, Optimizely, Adobe Target. They know the promise. They know the gap. They're looking for something that matches what they already believe: segments aren't enough.

# Key Messages

1. Every person is a market of one — the era of audiences is ending
2. The technology is invisible — your website just becomes relevant
3. It's effortless — one button, zero config, your entire site

# Calls to Action

- Primary: Request early access
- Secondary: How it works (→ editorial page)

# Tone

Full manifesto. Staccato fragments alternating with long-then-short contrast. Provocatively calm — big claims delivered without shouting. Serif italic accents in red on the emotional pivot word of each headline. The page speaks from conviction. It doesn't ask permission.

# Copy

## Section 1 — Hero

Full-viewport opening. Giant "ONE" as background watermark. The first thing the visitor reads is a declaration, not a product description.

- Eyebrow: Audience of One
- Headline: The end of *audiences.* / The beginning of *everyone.*
- Subhead: For decades, marketing meant shouting at segments. Categories. Cohorts. **We believe every person is a market of one.** The marketing nirvana is real — and it's simpler than you think.

## Section 2 — Manifesto statements

Three numbered declarations. Each one subverts an industry assumption. Short, punchy, one statement per row.

- 01: Segments are *a confession* that you don't know your customer well enough.
- 02: It's not a feature. It's the *absence of compromise.*
- 03: The best technology *disappears* behind the experience it creates.

## Section 3 — Metrics

Three numbers. No explanation needed — the numbers are the argument.

- 1 — Audience size that matters
- 0 — Segments required
- ∞ — Unique experiences

## Section 4 — Key line (pull quote)

Centered. Italic serif. The brand's defining sentence.

- Quote: "We made your website **pay attention** — so every visitor finds exactly what they came for, as if it had always been there."
- Attribution: of1.live

## Section 5 — Contrast (before / after)

Side-by-side. Same visitor, different experience. Make the marketer feel what their visitor feels.

- Eyebrow: The same visitor. A different website.
- Before label: Without of1
- Before headline: Your visitor arrives.
- Before body: They scroll past three sections that don't apply to them. They open the pricing page meant for enterprise when they're a startup. They leave. Your analytics say they "bounced."
- After label: With of1
- After headline: Your visitor arrives.
- After body: The page already knows what they're looking for. The case study matches their industry. The pricing reflects their size. They don't scroll — they read. They don't bounce — they stay.

## Section 6 — Why now (teaser)

A short, punchy bridge between the contrast and social proof. Manifesto rhythm — not explanatory prose. Links to the full vision page.

- Eyebrow: Why now
- Headline: Your visitors already know what *relevance* feels like.
- Body: Every day, they use tools that remember them — that know their preferences, their history, their intent. Tools that respond accordingly. Then they arrive on your website. Same page as everyone else. That gap has a name. It's called a *confession.*
- CTA: The vision → /why.html

## Section 8 — CTA
