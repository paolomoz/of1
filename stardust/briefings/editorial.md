# Intent

This is the depth page — for people who felt something on the landing page and want to understand. It reads like a long-form magazine piece: someone sits down with their coffee and reads the story of why the era of audiences is ending. Technical details are proof points woven into narrative, not the structure itself.

# Audience

Same persona as landing, but further along. They've seen the manifesto, they believe the premise, and now they want to know: how does this actually work? Is it real? They're reading with a mix of hope and healthy skepticism. Many are technical-adjacent — they won't build it themselves but they need to believe the architecture is sound.

# Key Messages

1. Generative websites are real — pages composed in real time for each visitor, not chatbots
2. Three simultaneous breakthroughs made this possible now
3. The visitor experiences relevance without noticing the technology
4. The pipeline is three stages, 2.5 seconds, non-negotiable performance
5. One button for the marketer — the complexity is the product's problem

# Calls to Action

- Primary: Request early access (end of page)
- Secondary: none — the page is a single reading experience

# Tone

Still manifesto at its core, but measured editorial prose. Longer sentences, flowing paragraphs, persuasive rhythm. Pull quotes break the narrative with punchy declarations. The conviction is constant; the cadence adapts for sustained reading.

# Copy

## Section 1 — Editorial hero

No full-viewport drama — this is a reading page. Clean headline, strong lede, then into the narrative.

- Eyebrow: of1 — Audience of ONE
- Headline: The era of audiences is ending. Here's what comes next.
- Lede: For twenty years, the web has worked backwards. We build pages for everyone and hope the right people find them. We sort visitors into segments and call it understanding. We've been confessing — to every visitor, on every page — that we don't know them well enough.

## Section 2 — The premise: websites that pay attention

The opening narrative. Establishes what a generative website is without using that term upfront. Paints the picture before naming the thing.

- Headline: What if your website could listen
- Body: Imagine a website that doesn't serve the same page to every visitor. Not a chatbot bolted on the corner. Not a recommendation widget at the bottom. The page itself — every headline, every case study, every call to action — considered for this person, at this moment.
- Body: The page your visitor sees isn't the page you published. It's the page they needed. And it arrives as if it had always been there.
- Pull quote: "Not a chatbot. Not a recommendation engine. A website — that happens to be **yours**."
- Body: This isn't science fiction. Three things changed, all at once — and they changed everything.

## Section 3 — Why now: three breakthroughs

Three cards. Each breakthrough is a technical truth delivered in human terms. The structure echoes the landing manifesto statements but with depth.

- Headline: Three breakthroughs. All at once.
- Card 01 — Inference moved to the edge: AI that runs in milliseconds at the CDN layer, not in a distant data center. Speed isn't a feature — it's the reason visitors don't notice.
- Card 02 — Models got fast enough to disappear: A year ago this would have taken seconds. Now it happens before the page renders. The gap between "authored" and "generated" closed.
- Card 03 — AI learned to stay on-brand: Models can now compose content that respects your voice, your design system, your guardrails. Not generic — yours.

## Section 4 — What the visitor experiences

Back to narrative. The visitor is the protagonist. Make the marketer feel what their visitor will feel.

- Headline: Your visitor doesn't know. That's the point.
- Body: The visitor arrives. The page makes sense to them. Not because they navigated perfectly — because the page already considered who they are. Less cognitive work. Relevant from the first interaction. Answers the question they actually have.
- Body: They don't feel targeted. They feel understood. There's a difference — and your visitors know it instantly.
- Body: If they look for the seams, they can find them. The page doesn't hide what it is. It just doesn't advertise it.
- Pull quote: "Most AI wants you to know it's there. Ours wants you to **forget**."

## Section 5 — How it works: the pipeline

Three-stage visual pipeline. The technical architecture made inevitable, not complex. The 2.5s stat is the anchor.

- Headline: Three stages. 2.5 seconds.
- Stage 1 — Understand: The system reads the visitor — not by tracking, but by listening. What they searched for, where they came from, what time it is. A portrait drawn in seconds.
- Stage 2 — Reason: Given what it knows, the system decides what to change. Not everything. Small shifts — a different headline, a reordered section, a case study swapped for one in their industry.
- Stage 3 — Compose: The page renders. 72 composable blocks. Every element considered. The visitor sees a website — that happens to be theirs. **2.5s**

## Section 6 — Two modes: explicit and implicit

Side-by-side contrast. Same pipeline, different trigger. The footer line ties them together.

- Eyebrow: Two modes. One pipeline.
- Explicit label: The visitor asks.
- Explicit body: They type a question, express an intent. The page recomposes around their request. Like search — but instead of ten blue links, they get a whole page.
- Implicit label: The site notices.
- Implicit body: No typing required. Behavioral signals, referral context, time of day. The page shifts — subtly, invisibly — to meet them where they are.
- Footer line: Both modes share the same pipeline. The difference is who starts the conversation.

## Section 7 — What the marketer gets

Back to narrative. The payoff for the buyer. Effortlessness is the message.

- Headline: One button. Your entire site.
- Body: Not a widget you embed. Not a recommendation engine bolted on. of1 works across your whole site — every page, every section. Enable it, and every visitor's experience improves.
- Body: No rules to write. No segments to define. No content matrix to maintain. The marketer's work is already done — the content is already on the site. of1 just decides which parts of it matter most to each person.

## Section 8 — The speed constraint

A standalone section that makes the engineering discipline feel like a brand value. The callout stat is the visual anchor.

- Headline: 2.5 seconds. Or it doesn't ship.
- Body: Speed is not a feature of of1. It's a constraint that shapes everything. If the page takes longer than 2.5 seconds to render, the visitor notices. And if they notice, the technology isn't invisible — it's in the way.
- Body: Edge inference, aggressive caching, progressive rendering. The engineering is brutal because the experience must be effortless.
- Callout: LCP: 2.5s — Maximum. Not average.
