Visual motifs

High-contrast editorial minimalism

Generous negative space interrupted by bold typographic statements. Dark mode dominant with warm cream accents. No gradients, no glassmorphism, no safe corporate blue.

Foundational layout principle. Every section breathes. Content is the ornament.

Signal Fire palette

The combination of black, cream, and red evokes urgency and conviction, like a signal fire in darkness.

The three-color core (black/cream/red) should dominate. Blue and mint are punctuation, not co-stars.

Serif italic accent

Single words in Cormorant Garamond italic + red within mono or display headlines. The literary voice breaking through the engineered voice.

One word per headline max. The italic word is the emotional pivot. e.g. "The end of audiences."

Pill-shaped elements

Buttons and tags use border-radius: 100px — fully rounded capsules. Tone tags, CTAs, and category labels all share this shape.

All interactive elements and classification tags. Never square or slightly-rounded buttons.

Faint dividers

Section borders at rgba(245,240,232,0.06) — barely visible, felt more than seen.

Between all major sections. The brand relies on negative space and faint structure, not heavy rules.
Photography & imagery
Abstract, high-contrast, editorial. Dark environments with warm light. No stock photography of smiling people at laptops.

Do

  • Abstract/conceptual imagery over literal product screenshots
  • Dark backgrounds consistent with brand palette
  • People silhouetted or partially obscured — the visitor, not the marketer
  • Warm color temperature — cream, amber, fire tones against dark

Don't

  • Corporate stock photography
  • Bright white backgrounds
  • Group shots of diverse teams pointing at screens
  • Literal AI imagery (robots, neural networks, glowing brains)

Tone adaptation
Context-dependent. Marketing surfaces use manifesto rhythm — staccato declarations, long-then-short contrasts, single italic accent words. Editorial and documentation use measured, precise prose. The conviction is constant; the cadence adapts.

To provoke

Reframe the familiar. Make the reader question what they accepted. "Segments are a confession."

To intimate

Speak to one person, not an audience. The irony is intentional — a brand about one-to-one speaks one-to-one.

To simplify

Complex technology described in human terms. What it feels like, not how it works. "Your site pays attention."

Clever

Headlines, taglines, hero copy, social cards. The manifesto voice. Rhythm and subversion.

Clear

Product explanations, documentation, onboarding. Precise prose. No clever for its own sake.

Content pillars

The death of impersonal

The philosophical position — why segments, cohorts, and audiences are an admission of failure.

Invisible technology

The product truth — AI that disappears behind the experience. No badges, no spinners, no novelty.

The visitor's experience

Always told through the end user's eyes. The marketer is the buyer, but the visitor is the protagonist.

Effortless for everyone

Zero config for the brand, zero friction for the visitor. Complexity is the product's problem, not the customer's.
